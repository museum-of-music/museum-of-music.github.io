A virtual museum where every exhibit tells the true story behind music's most famous objects. This time: a guitar that hung on a restaurant wall before it broke every auction record, a wall built just to be knocked down, and a pig that went wildly off script.

The Guitar Room

David Gilmour's Black Strat

1969 Fender Stratocaster · bought 1970

Bought off the shelf in a New York music shop and played on the classic Pink Floyd albums, including the solo on “Comfortably Numb.” Then it spent about eleven years hanging on a restaurant wall, next to the burgers.

The Demolition Room

The Wall

Cardboard bricks · The Wall tour, 1980–81

Every night, the crew built a wall between the band and the audience, brick by brick, until the band disappeared behind it. At the end of the show, they knocked the whole thing down. On purpose. Then they built it again the next night.

The Hidden Wing

Algie, the flying pig

40-foot inflatable pig · December 1976

A marksman was hired to shoot the pig down if it ever broke loose during the photo shoot for the Animals album cover. On the one day it did, nobody had booked him. It drifted into the skies over Heathrow and landed on a farm in Kent.

About this museum

A museum you won't find on a map

The Museum of Music is a fictional museum. Please don't look for us on a map (you'll end up in a cornfield). The objects shown are digital recreations, not the real items, but every story, fact, and auction price is real.

This site is not affiliated with or endorsed by the artists, their estates, or any organizations featured. We're just music fans, grateful to the artists for the music that made these stories worth telling.
