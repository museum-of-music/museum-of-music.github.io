The Beatles: “The Magical Mystery Tour”

A virtual museum where every exhibit tells the true story behind music's most famous objects. Step inside for a guided tour of three Beatles artifacts, the history behind them, and the auction records they set.

In the galleries

Three artifacts, three true stories

Each room of the exhibition holds one object. Here are the gallery labels.

The Drum Room

Ringo Starr's drop-T drum head

Ludwig bass drum head · 1964

Played on The Beatles' first appearance on The Ed Sullivan Show, watched by about 73 million people. The logo was sketched on a scrap of paper in a London drum shop and painted by hand for about $14.

The Outfit Room

The Sgt. Pepper uniforms

Satin military-style suits · 1967

Stage clothes for a band that didn't exist. The Beatles invented Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band after they stopped touring, and some of the medals pinned to these jackets are real.

The Hidden Wing

The Abbey Road crossing

Zebra crossing, London · 8 August 1969

A policeman held the traffic for about ten minutes while photographer Iain Macmillan stood on a stepladder and took six frames. It's the one Beatles artifact the public can still walk across.

About this museum

A museum you won't find on a map

The Museum of Music is a fictional museum. Please don't look for us on a map (you'll end up in a cornfield). The objects shown are digital recreations, not the real items, but every story, fact, and auction price is real.

This site is not affiliated with or endorsed by the artists, their estates, or any organizations featured. We're just music fans, grateful to the artists for the music that made these stories worth telling.
